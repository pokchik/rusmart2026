About the Workshop

The rapid growth of IoT devices and smart-space infrastructure has expanded the attack surface exposed to malicious actors, while growing reliance on AI for autonomous decision-making introduces new classes of vulnerability — from adversarial manipulation to data poisoning.

WSIS brings together researchers, engineers and practitioners working across cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and IoT-enabled smart systems. The workshop's premise is simple: intelligence and security should be co-designed, not treated as separate concerns bolted onto a finished system.

Original contributions are welcome reporting theoretical advances, system designs, empirical studies and position papers — from early-career researchers and established groups alike.

Topics of Interest
Foundations & Architectures Reference models for secure intelligent systems; trust, identity and access management; distributed, edge and fog architectures; formal verification and simulation; standards, interoperability and benchmarking
AI for Security ML/DL for intrusion and anomaly detection; AI-driven threat intelligence; federated learning for private security; explainable AI (XAI) for security; automated incident response
Security for AI Adversarial ML and model robustness; data poisoning and model extraction defenses; trustworthy, certifiable AI; privacy-preserving ML; AI supply-chain and MLOps security
Secure & Intelligent IoT Lightweight cryptography and authentication; secure sensor networks and device management; resilience and self-healing infrastructure; blockchain / DLT for IoT trust; digital twins for security simulation
Data, Networks & Infrastructure Big data analytics for secure systems; 5G/6G and next-generation network security; cloud, edge and hybrid infrastructure; data quality, provenance and governance
Domain-Specific Applications Smart homes, buildings and cities; smart healthcare and e-health; connected and autonomous vehicles; smart energy and industrial IoT; agriculture, education, logistics
Human & Governance Aspects Human factors and user trust; ethics, fairness and accountability; legal and regulatory perspectives; education and workforce development
Submission Guidelines
  • Prepare — format your paper in Springer LNCS style. Full papers up to 12 pages, short/position papers up to 6 pages.
  • Submit — submissions are handled through EasyChair, under the WSIS workshop track.
  • Review — each submission undergoes double-blind review by the WSIS Technical Program Committee.
  • Present — accepted papers are published in the workshop proceedings and presented online.
